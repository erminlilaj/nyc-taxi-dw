01 / Objective

02

The question

Raw trip records are not yet analytical evidence.

Taxi operations create millions of detailed records. We needed one reliable model that turns them into repeatable questions about demand, revenue, payments, vendors, and city movement.

Why a warehouse

Consistent definitions

Measures and dimensions are defined once, so every query uses the same cleaned business logic.

Why OLAP

Repeatable questions

SQL supports the same drill-down and comparison questions without manual recalculation.

Why a graph

Visible connections

Aggregated routes reveal how locations, payments, vendors, and demand are related.

Project goal: move from isolated source rows to a governed analytical model that is simple to query, validate, and explain.

Source and scope

A focused three-month dataset makes the project testable end to end.

The source combines trip-level operational data with a Taxi Zone Lookup table. It is rich enough to study time, geography, payment, service, and revenue while remaining reproducible on a local analytical stack.

Why this scope? Q1 2024 provides seasonal and weekday/weekend variation without hiding the full ETL and validation workflow behind an oversized sample.

03 / Dataset

05

Data quality controls

Cleaning rules protect every conclusion made later.

  1. 01Keep pickups in the defined January–March 2024 window.
  2. 02Reject missing timestamps or missing pickup/drop-off locations.
  3. 03Remove non-positive or implausible fares and distances.
  4. 04Validate passenger counts and trip durations from 1 to 300 minutes.
  5. 05Normalize missing tip values to zero before aggregation.

8.45 million cleaned trips form the analytical base.

Without these controls, invalid distances, fares, timestamps, or locations would distort rankings, averages, and route aggregates.

Why load dimensions first? Facts can then reference validated, reusable dimension keys and PostgreSQL can enforce the warehouse contract.

04 / Warehouse design

06

Dimensional Fact Model

One fact row represents one cleaned taxi trip.

Why this model

Trip grain preserves flexibility.

Every measure remains traceable to a single trip while dimensions support aggregation at day, zone, borough, payment, and vendor levels.

Role-playing locations matter.

Separate pickup and drop-off dimensions let the same zone act as an origin or a destination without ambiguity.

05 / OLAP analysis

09

Reproducibility and validation

The analysis can be rebuilt, checked, and explained.

Why traceability

Executable pipeline

Download, clean, dimensional load, OLAP export, graph aggregation, and graph loading are all project steps.

Why checks

Warehouse validation

Fact and dimension row counts, keys, and constraints verify that loading completed correctly.

Why code-generated charts

Reproducible figures

Charts come from Python queries against PostgreSQL rather than manually edited screenshots.

Why a derived graph

Auditable relationships

Neo4j aggregates are regenerated from PostgreSQL, preserving a route back to the source of truth.

Result: another reviewer can follow the same scripts and recover the warehouse, figures, and graph from the same data inputs.
